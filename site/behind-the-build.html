THE WORKFLOW

A useful loop:
direct, inspect, refine.

01 / DEFINE

Start with a real problem.

What would make the workflow useful? A stopped miner needs trustworthy state. A paper account needs an auditable result. A fitness reading needs a source and time. I set the objective and the constraints before accepting a screen.

02 / BUILD

Make the result reviewable.

I direct, coordinate, test and manage development: I define the problems, review results and decide what to accept or refine. AI tools perform code generation and assist with research, documentation and test preparation. The portfolio distinguishes my decisions and validation from work performed by those tools.

03 / DEBUG

Investigate the failure.

A phone URL exposed a routing assumption. Candle reconciliation revealed unsupported data. A journal needed crash recovery. A watch handoff failed. Those failures are part of the engineering record, not details to erase from the story.

04 / VERIFY

Match the claim to the evidence.

A screenshot proves appearance. A unit test proves a bounded case. A Simulator test proves behavior in that environment. Device acceptance and current production operation need their own evidence. I keep those milestones separate.

TOOLS + DISCIPLINES

Different stacks.
Shared habits.

The reviewed materials include Python, React and TypeScript, SwiftUI and SwiftData, HealthKit reads, CoreBluetooth, Zepp OS JavaScript, journaled records and source-controlled tests.

They demonstrate project work across software and hardware. They are not a claim of employment history, credentials or universal expertise.

Source-aware data

Preserve identity, units, timestamps, freshness and missing states before presenting a number.

Isolated validation

Use synthetic data and explicit review checkpoints. Never make a portfolio preview a control surface for production.

Recovery and rollback

Consider interruption, duplicate requests, ownership and preserved checkpoints. A successful request is not always a successful outcome.

Privacy by selection

Share reviewed summaries and sanitized assets. Keep full records, private repositories and personal data outside the published surface.

READING THE STATUS LABELS

Evidence has a scope.

Approved design

A selected visual direction. It may be artwork or a simulated interface rather than a working operational path.

Implemented

Source contains the behavior. That alone does not prove a physical device, current deployment or complete test suite.

Recorded validation

A dated test or observation supports a named checkpoint. The environment and known failures remain part of the result.

Experimental

An investigation or integration exists but important device, meaning, delivery or reliability gates remain.

Planned

A documented intention or design. It is not presented as a completed feature.

Unknown or unverified

Available evidence does not establish the claim. The right response is to name the gap and define the next check.
